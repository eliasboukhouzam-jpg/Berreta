# 02 — Master timeline: weapons breakthroughs and their materials

> Brainstorm draft, 13 Oct 2026. About 200 breakthroughs in 11 eras.
> **Legend:** untagged = 🟡 textbook consensus · ✅ = hard evidence (artifact, document, official data) · ⚠️ = estimate, contested or single-source: **verify before publishing**.
> Columns: **When** · **Breakthrough** · **Where** · **Why it mattered** · **Key materials** · **Production chain (one line)**.
> ★ = proposed flagship deep dive (see `01-concept.md` §9).

## Era 0: Stone, bone and wood (2.6 million years ago → c. 5000 BCE)

*Story of the era:* people can only use what they can pick up, and the first "strategic mineral" is a sharp rock.

| When | Breakthrough | Where | Why it mattered | Key materials | Chain |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| c. 2.6 Mya | Oldowan flaked stone tools | Gona, Ethiopia | First deliberately made sharp edges. Tools first, weapons by extension | Basalt, quartzite, chert | Pick a suitable cobble → strike flakes with a hammerstone |
| c. 1.76 Mya | Acheulean hand axe | Kokiselei, Kenya | One standard design used for ~1.5 million years | Flint, chert, quartzite, obsidian | Select stone → shape both faces by knapping |
| c. 500,000 BP | Hafted stone spear points ⚠️ | Kathu Pan, South Africa | First **composite** weapon: stone + wood + binding | Banded ironstone/chert, wood, sinew | Knap point → shape shaft → bind/glue |
| c. 300,000–200,000 BP | Schöningen wooden spears ⚠️ (dating revised c. 2025, verify) | Lower Saxony, Germany | Oldest complete hunting weapons known | Spruce, pine | Fell sapling → debark → carve point from the hard base |
| c. 200,000 BP | Birch-tar adhesive | Campitello, Italy (Neanderthals) | Arguably the **first synthetic material**: heat transforms bark into glue | Birch bark | Heat bark without oxygen → collect tar → haft points |
| c. 64,000 BP | Bow and arrow | Sibudu Cave, South Africa | Killing at a distance with little effort | Quartz/dolerite points, wood, sinew/plant-fibre string, ochre-based glue | Gather wood & fibre → shape stave → knap microliths → glue & bind |
| c. 54,000 BP | Earliest bow use in Europe ⚠️ | Grotte Mandrin, France | Bows used by *Homo sapiens* entering Europe | Flint microlith points | As above |
| c. 30,000–10,000 BP | Boomerang / throwing stick | Australia (Wyrie Swamp ~10,000 BP); older ivory example in Poland ⚠️ | Aerodynamic hunting/fighting weapon | Hardwood, mammoth ivory | Carve from curved branch/root |
| c. 20,000–17,000 BP | Spear-thrower (atlatl) | France, Spain | Lever roughly doubles throwing speed | Reindeer antler, wood | Carve antler hook → attach to dart shaft |
| c. 13,400 BP | Jebel Sahaba cemetery ✅ (context) | Sudan | Earliest evidence of repeated violence between groups | Stone projectile fragments in bones | (No chain. Evidence of use) |
| c. 11,000 BP onward | Obsidian trade | Melos → Franchthi Cave (Greece); Anatolia → Levant | **First long-distance trade in a weapons mineral.** Obsidian edges are sharper than steel | Obsidian (volcanic glass) | Quarry at volcanic source → carry/sail hundreds of km → knap at destination |
| c. 10,000 BP | Nataruk massacre ✅ (context) | Turkana, Kenya | Early evidence of organized group killing | Obsidian bladelets embedded in skulls | — |
| c. 8,000–6,000 BCE | Sling ⚠️ (dating hard) | Near East | Cheap and long-range; outranges early bows | Wool/flax/plant cord; clay or stone shot | Braid cord + pouch; fire clay bullets |
| c. 8,000 BCE | Cold-hammered native copper | Çayönü, Anatolia | First metal shaping (beads/awls, not yet weapons) | Native copper | Find nuggets → hammer & anneal |
| c. 6000–3000 BCE | Stone mace | Near East, Egypt | Among the first weapons designed purely to fight people | Diorite, granite, limestone + wood | Peck/grind stone head → drill hole → haft |
| c. 5000 BCE | First copper smelting | Belovode, Serbia | Start of metallurgy: rock becomes metal | Malachite & azurite ores, charcoal | Mine oxide ore → smelt in small furnace with charcoal & blowpipes |
| c. 3300 BCE | ★ Ötzi's kit ✅ | Ötztal Alps | A full **"loadout"** preserved: copper axe, flint dagger, yew bow, arrows | Copper (~99.7%, ore traced to Tuscany ⚠️), flint, yew, viburnum, birch tar, sinew | Ores and flint traveled hundreds of km before reaching one man |
| c. 3100 BCE | Narmer Palette ✅ (context) | Egypt | King shown smiting with a mace: weapon becomes a symbol of state | — | — |

## Era 1: Bronze Age (c. 3300 → 1200 BCE)

*Story of the era:* the first global supply chain. Copper is common, tin is rare, and bronze needs both, so whoever controls tin routes controls weapons.

| When | Breakthrough | Where | Why it mattered | Key materials | Chain |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| c. 4000–3000 BCE | Arsenical bronze | Near East, Caucasus, Iberia | Harder than copper; the first alloy | Copper + arsenic (from arsenic-rich ores) | Smelt mixed ores; toxic fumes (the "lame smith god" idea is speculation ⚠️) |
| c. 3000–2500 BCE | Tin bronze | Mesopotamia, Anatolia; Royal Cemetery of Ur (~2600 BCE) | ~90% Cu / 10% Sn, harder and castable. **Needs long-distance tin** | Copper; tin (cassiterite) from Central Asia (Uzbekistan/Tajikistan), Anatolia ⚠️, later Cornwall, Iberia, Brittany, Erzgebirge | Mine Cu → smelt → ingots; pan tin from streams → smelt → trade both → melt & alloy at destination |
| c. 2450 BCE | Stele of the Vultures ✅ | Lagash, Sumer | Earliest depiction of a phalanx with helmets, spears, battle-wagons | Copper/bronze spearheads, helmets | City-temple workshops |
| c. 2400–2000 BCE | Socketed bronze axes & spearheads | Near East → Europe | Strong hafting; mass-castable | Bronze | Two-piece stone/clay molds; lost-wax |
| c. 2100–1800 BCE | Spoked-wheel chariot | Sintashta culture (Urals) | Speed + mobile archery platform; dominant weapon system for ~800 years | Bent birch/elm/ash wood, leather, bronze fittings, **horses** | Wheelwrights, carpenters, leatherworkers, horse breeders & trainers (Kikkuli text, c. 1350 BCE) |
| c. 2000 BCE onward | ★ Composite bow | Steppe & Near East | Short, powerful bow usable from a chariot/horse | Wood core, horn belly, sinew back, hide/fish glue | Months to a year of curing; Tutankhamun buried with dozens ✅ |
| c. 1900–1500 BCE | Khopesh (sickle-sword) | Egypt / Canaan | Evolved from the axe; first iconic "sword" shape in Egypt | Bronze | Cast + hammer-hardened edge |
| c. 1700 BCE | Aegean rapier | Minoan Crete, Mycenae | Long thrusting blade; weak riveted hilts | Bronze | Cast long blade; organic hilt riveted |
| c. 1450–1400 BCE | Dendra panoply ✅ | Mycenaean Greece | Full bronze body armor, 15 plates | Bronze sheet; boar's-tusk helmet (dozens of boars per helmet ⚠️) | Sheet-hammering; tusk plates sewn to leather |
| c. 1323 BCE | Tutankhamun's meteoritic iron dagger ✅ | Egypt | "Iron from the sky," worth more than gold | Iron-nickel meteorite (~11% Ni) | Forged from a meteorite. No smelting |
| c. 1305 BCE | Uluburun shipwreck ✅ | Off southern Turkey | **The supply chain caught in the act:** ~10 t copper (354 oxhide ingots) + ~1 t tin, the exact bronze ratio | Copper (Cyprus), tin (Central Asia and/or western sources ⚠️), glass, ebony | Sea trade around the eastern Mediterranean |
| c. 1300 BCE | ★ Naue II sword | Central Europe → whole Mediterranean | First robust cut-and-thrust sword; template that outlived bronze | Bronze ~88–90% Cu, 10–12% Sn; organic grip | See worked example |
| 1274 BCE | Battle of Kadesh ✅ (context) | Syria | Largest chariot battle (~5,000+ chariots ⚠️) | — | Egyptian & Hittite state chariot workshops |
| c. 1200–1150 BCE | Late Bronze Age collapse (context) | Eastern Mediterranean | Trade networks break; **tin supply disrupted** → iron (local) gains ground ⚠️ (one thesis among several) | — | — |

## Era 2: Iron Age and Classical antiquity (c. 1200 BCE → 500 CE)

*Story of the era:* iron ore is everywhere, so more people can be armed. Now the scarce inputs are **charcoal (forests), skilled smiths, and money**.

| When | Breakthrough | Where | Why it mattered | Key materials | Chain |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| c. 1200–1000 BCE | Bloomery iron & steeling | Anatolia, Levant, Cyprus → everywhere | Ore is common → **democratized weapons** | Iron ore (hematite, magnetite, limonite, bog iron); **huge amounts of charcoal** | Smelt in clay bloomery (no melting) → hammer out slag → carburize → quench & temper |
| c. 1st millennium BCE | Sub-Saharan iron smelting ⚠️ (independent invention debated) | Nok (Nigeria), Termit (Niger), Great Lakes | Iron weapons and tools across Africa | Laterite ores, charcoal | Natural-draft & bellows furnaces |
| c. 900–600 BCE | Assyrian war machine | Mesopotamia | First iron-armed standing army; siege rams, lamellar armor, cavalry | Iron (Sargon II's palace stored ~160 t of iron ⚠️) | Tribute & imperial workshops |
| c. 700–650 BCE | Hoplite panoply | Greece | Citizen-soldier heavy infantry | Bronze (helmet, shield facing), linen (linothorax), ash wood (dory), iron spearheads | Household purchase: soldiers bought their own kit |
| 483–480 BCE | ★ Laurion silver → Athenian trireme fleet ✅ | Attica | A silver strike funds ~200 triremes → Salamis. **Money as a weapons mineral** | Silver/lead (galena); fir, pine, oak; flax rope; pitch; bronze ram | Enslaved miners at Laurion → state treasury → shipyards at Piraeus |
| 5th–3rd c. BCE | ★ Chinese crossbow (nu) | Warring States China | Lethal with minimal training; bronze trigger mechanism | Bronze trigger, wood/horn prod, silk/sinew string | Qin standardized triggers & arrowheads; ~40,000 arrowheads with Terracotta Army ⚠️, inscribed with workshop names (early quality control) ✅ |
| 5th c. BCE | Cast iron & blast furnace | China | ~1,500 years before Europe; mass tools and weapons | Iron ore, charcoal (later coal) | Liquid iron cast in molds; decarburized into steel |
| 399 BCE | Gastraphetes ("belly bow") | Syracuse (Dionysius I) | First known **state weapons R&D program** | Wood, horn, sinew, bronze | Tyrant gathers engineers into a workshop |
| c. 350–340 BCE | Torsion catapult/ballista | Macedon (Philip II) | Artillery powered by twisted springs | **Sinew or hair** springs, wood, bronze/iron fittings | Carthaginian women's hair donated for springs (legend ⚠️) |
| c. 338 BCE | Sarissa pike | Macedon | 4–6 m pike makes the phalanx a wall | Cornel wood, iron head/butt | Long straight-grained shafts were scarce |
| c. 300 BCE onward | ★ Wootz crucible steel → "Damascus" blades | South India & Sri Lanka → Persia, Syria (~900–1750 CE) | Ultra-high-carbon steel with watered pattern | Iron + carbon in sealed crucibles; trace **vanadium/molybdenum** from specific ores | Recipe lost c. 1750–1800 (likely depletion of ore with right impurities 🟡) |
| c. 3rd c. BCE | Mail armor | Celts | Flexible iron armor for ~1,500 years | Drawn iron wire, 20,000–50,000 rings ⚠️ | Draw wire → coil → cut → rivet each ring |
| c. 200 BCE–100 CE | ★ Roman gladius & pilum | Rome | Short stabbing sword + javelin whose soft shank bends | **Noric steel** (Noricum, Austria; manganese-rich ores ⚠️) | Provincial mines → legionary workshops |
| 117 BCE | Han iron monopoly ✅ | China | State controls iron & salt, funds wars vs Xiongnu | Iron, salt | State foundries |
| c. 1st–4th c. CE | Roman *fabricae* | Roman Empire | ~35 state arms factories (Notitia Dignitatum ⚠️) = first military-industrial network | Iron, bronze, leather, wood | Specialized factories per item (shields, armor, artillery) |
| c. 302–322 CE | Stirrup (enabler) | China (Jin dynasty) | Stable rider, later enables shock cavalry | Iron/bronze | Reaches Europe ~8th c. |

|---|---|---|---|---|---|

## Era 4: Gunpowder revolution (1450 → 1800)

*Story of the era:* guns become infantry weapons. The new chokepoints are **saltpeter, gunflint, bronze/iron for cannon, oak for navies, and money (American silver)**.

## Era 6: World War I (1914 → 1918)

*Story of the era:* total war means the whole economy is the weapon. Shortages of nitrates, copper, acetone, rubber and tungsten shape strategy.
