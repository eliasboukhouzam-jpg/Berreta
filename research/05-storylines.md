# 05 — Storylines: the threads that pull a reader through 5,000 years

> Brainstorm draft, 13 Oct 2026. A timeline is a list. **Storylines are what make people keep reading.** Each one is a guided path that jumps across eras and links weapon pages and material pages.
> Proposed format per storyline: 8–12 "stops", each stop = one weapon or material page + a 2-sentence bridge.

---

## 1. Saltpeter: the 1,000-year nitrogen war ★ (strongest story)
Chinese alchemists (c. 850) → nitrous earth scraped from stables → Lavoisier's Régie des Poudres (1775) and the French saltpeter campaign → British East India Company's Bihar monopoly → Chile's **War of the Pacific** (1879–84) over nitrates → Germany's blockade problem (1914) → **Haber-Bosch** (1913) → ammonium nitrate IEDs (2000s) → 155 mm shells and natural-gas prices (2022–26).
*Hook:* the same element that feeds half the world's people makes almost every explosive.

## 2. Tin routes: the first strategic mineral
Copper is common, tin is rare → Central Asian & Cornish tin → Uluburun wreck → Late Bronze Age collapse → iron's rise → tin in today's solder (Myanmar's Wa State, DRC) → China's dominance.
*Hook:* the first time a civilization's weapons depended on a mine thousands of km away.

## 3. Forests to fossil fuels: the energy behind every weapon
Charcoal for bronze & iron (deforestation of Cyprus, Weald, Alps) → yew stripped from Europe for longbows → oak for ships of the line, Broad Arrow pines → coal & coke (Krupp, Ruhr) → **oil** (Churchill's 1912 switch, Anglo-Persian Oil) → synthetic fuel (Leuna) → jet fuel → Li-ion batteries for drones.

## 4. Who controls the rock controls the war: chokepoints
Laurion silver → Potosí → Bihar saltpeter → Chilean nitrate → Swedish iron ore (WWII) → Portuguese **wolfram** → **Shinkolobwe** uranium → Soviet titanium for the SR-71 → China's rare earths (2010 Japan, 2023–26 controls) → DFARS 2027.
*Hook:* every era has a material that one country holds, and the others scramble.

## 5. Craft to factory to code: how weapons are made
Knapping → mold casting → Qin standardized arrowheads → Roman *fabricae* → Venetian Arsenal → Honoré Blanc / Harpers Ferry interchangeable parts → Colt & Krupp → Ford-style tank lines (Tankograd) → stamped AK receivers → CNC & composites (F-35's 1,900+ suppliers ⚠️) → Ukrainian garage drone workshops → Stuxnet (a weapon that is only code).

## 6. Mass vs precision
Massed archers vs knights → volley fire → Maxim gun → WWI artillery → WWII mass production → precision era (Paveway, JDAM, 1991) → return of mass (Ukraine shells, Shahed swarms, FPVs) → cost-exchange problem ($50k drone vs $1m+ interceptor ⚠️) → lasers.

## 7. Armor vs weapon: the eternal race
Boar's-tusk helmet → bronze panoply → mail → plate (Milan/Augsburg) → bullets beat plate → ironclads → nickel-steel vs capped shells → tank armor vs shaped charge → Chobham & DU → Kevlar & boron carbide → drone cages on tanks ("cope cages", 2022–).

## 8. Who paid: the human supply chain
Laurion slaves → Potosí *mita* & mercury → Birmingham guns for the slave trade → Chilean nitrate workers (Santa María school massacre, 1907 ✅) → WWI "canary girls" (TNT jaundice) → Mittelbau-Dora (V-2) → Auschwitz-Monowitz (Buna) → Navajo uranium miners → Hanford & Oak Ridge contamination → DRC cobalt & coltan → Ukraine's mined farmland.
*Tone rule:* facts and numbers, no moralizing. The facts are heavy enough.

## 9. Weapons that changed borders
Bronze-tin trade & empires → iron & the Assyrians → stirrup & feudalism (contested "Great Stirrup Controversy" ⚠️) → gunpowder empires (Ottoman, Safavid, Mughal) → Maxim gun & the Scramble for Africa → Adwa 1896 (Ethiopia defeats Italy with modern rifles) → the bomb & the Cold War map.

## 10. The arms company that never died (Beretta thread, optional brand-neutral)
Gardone 1526 → Venetian Republic → Napoleonic era → Italian unification → world wars → Beretta 92 / US M9 (1985) → 500 years (2026). Use this to show **continuity of place and skill**, without becoming a brand page (see name risk, `01-concept.md` §11).

## 11. Arms control: the long attempt to say "no"
Lateran II (1139, crossbows ⚠️) → Japanese gun restrictions under the Tokugawa (debated ⚠️) → Hague 1899 (dum-dum bullets) → Geneva Protocol 1925 → NPT 1968 → BWC 1972 → CWC 1993 → Ottawa 1997 → Cluster Munitions 2008 → autonomous weapons talks (2020s) → **export controls on minerals (2023–26) as the new arms control, in reverse.**
