# 06 — Sources: where the content will come from

> Brainstorm draft, 13 Oct 2026. Rule: **Wikipedia is a map, never a source.** Every published claim cites one of the tiers below.

## Tier 1: Primary data & official sources
- **USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries** (annual; 2026 edition released 6 Feb 2026): mineral production shares, US import reliance. <https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/mcs2026>
- **Congressional Research Service**: e.g. *Rare Earth Elements in National Defense* (R41744, Grasso 2013), defense industrial base reports.
- **SIPRI**: Arms Transfers Database, Top 100 arms-producing companies, Military Expenditure Database.
- **British Geological Survey**: World Mineral Statistics, critical minerals lists.
- **EU Critical Raw Materials Act** lists; **IEA** Global Critical Minerals Outlook.
- **China MOFCOM** export-control announcements (primary text for every control date).
- **Conflict Armament Research**: field traces of components inside weapons (Shahed, Russian missiles).
- **Museum collection databases**: Royal Armouries, The Met (Arms & Armor), British Museum, Smithsonian NASM, Imperial War Museums, Musée de l'Armée, Deutsches Historisches Museum.
- Archives: e.g. Archivio di Stato di Venezia (Beretta 1526 receipt).

## Source hygiene rules
1. Every number gets a year and a source. Mineral shares change annually.
2. Press releases from junior mining companies are **not** sources for market data (they talk their book).
3. Contested claims are shown as contested on the page, not silently picked.
4. Live-policy boxes (export controls, DFARS) show a "last checked" date.
