## 0. The rule: borrow the craft, not the voice or the assets

Anduril and Beretta are **sales sites**. Their job is to make weapons desirable. Ours is to explain them. So:

| Borrow ✅ | Don't borrow ❌ |
|---|---|
| Product-as-hero layouts, exploded views, process sequences, heritage timelines, restraint in imagery | Marketing euphemisms ("effectors", "defeat", "neutralize"). We say *destroy* and *kill* when that's what happens |
| How they make a **factory** a character in the story | Company claims stated as fact. They get an **L2 "company-reported"** badge (see `07-value-chain-model.md`) |
| Clean spec grids | Their renders, photos, 3D files, logos. All copyrighted/trademarked; we make or license our own |
| — | Glamour without cost. Every hero shot sits next to the "Who paid" section |

## 1. Site-by-site: what each one teaches

### Anduril (anduril.com): the modern-weapons half
- **What it does well**
  - Products grouped **by domain (air, land, sea)**, with **Lattice** software shown as the layer under everything 🟡 (search, Anduril product pages).
  - Each product has one short purpose line, e.g. Fury: *"autonomous air vehicle… near-fighter-speed performance, modular mission payloads, and affordable mass production"* ✅ (anduril.com/fury, via search).
  - Dark, minimal, cinematic: one object, lots of negative space, few specs *(memory ⚠️)*.
  - **The factory as a story:** Arsenal-1 (Pickaway County, near Rickenbacker, Columbus, Ohio). Announced at ~$1 bn, ~5 million sq ft, 4,000+ jobs. **First Ohio-built Fury rolled off the line 27 Jul 2026**, line capacity "up to 150 aircraft a year" ✅ (JobsOhio release = company/state-reported, L2).
  - Manifesto framing: "rebuild the arsenal," "affordable mass" *(memory ⚠️)*.
- **What we take:** domain filter for the 2014–2026 section; product-as-hero for T1 cards; **"software layer under hardware"** as a visual idea for Shift 4/7; the factory page as part of every value chain.
- **What we avoid:** the mission voice. We cite their claims, we don't adopt their worldview.

### Beretta (beretta.com): the heritage half
- **What it does well**
  - A **500-year lineage** told as continuity: Beretta's own framing is that 1526 is *"not a monument to the past, but evidence of a lineage that never stopped moving"* ✅ (company statement via all4shooters). **15th generation**, still in Gardone Val Trompia ✅.
  - **Place as protagonist:** the valley, the workers, the community. The 500th celebration on 18 Sep 2026 (with PM Meloni) was framed as "more than a celebration held behind factory walls" ✅.
  - **Open-doors storytelling:** 2026 promise to "reveal the secrets hidden in its long history" ✅.
  - Craft close-ups: steel, walnut, engraving, hands at work *(memory ⚠️)*.
- **What we take:** heritage tone for the **brief past** (12 revolutions); **macro texture photography of materials** (steel grain, bronze patina, walnut, flint) as the visual link to minerals; "place as character" for value-chain Place pages (Val Trompia, Bushveld, Atacama…).
- **What we avoid:** brand-page drift. Beretta appears as **one thread**, not as the site's sponsor (name risk, `01-concept.md` §11).

### Helsing: tone of restraint
- Its brand agency deliberately rejected "cliched, obvious imagery" in favor of "refinement and finesse" ✅ (Spin case study).
- **What we take:** **no stock explosions, no camo clichés, no "tactical" fonts.** Seriousness over spectacle.

## 3. Drawing models: the visual system

### 3.1 Visual style follows the evidence level
| Section | Style | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Brief past** (12 revolutions + 200 cards) | **Monochrome line illustrations** in one consistent hand (engraving/technical-drawing feel) | Fast to produce, timeless, matches the heritage tone; one style unifies 2.6 million years |
| **Deep present** (2014–2026) | **3D models** (rotatable, explodable) | Depth matches the detailed section |
| **Value chains** | **Maps + flow diagrams** (Sankey-style flows from country → refinery → factory) | Shows tonnage and shares at a glance |
| **Classified / unknown internals** | Same 3D, **marked "Reconstruction"** | Honesty: we don't know what's inside an F-35 radar |

### 3.2 The signature interaction: the exploded view *is* the value chain
1. The 3D model explodes into parts (Apple-style scroll).
2. Each part shows its material (slide → stainless steel).
3. Tap the material → its elements (chromium, nickel, iron).
4. Tap an element → the chain: **mine → country → tonnes → refinery → route → politics** (the 10 layers).

That joins the "drawing models" and the value chain into one experience. Nobody else does this. [Likely, from earlier competitor search]

### 3.3 v1 model list (proposed)
- **12 line illustrations** (revolutions): bow, bronze sword, chariot, iron sword, bombard, arquebus, ship of the line, Maxim gun, Mark I tank + biplane, radar/magnetron, Fat Man / ICBM, JDAM / Predator.
- **10 3D models** (modern): FPV drone, Shahed-136, TB2, glide-bomb kit, naval drone (Magura-type), Javelin, 155 mm shell, SIG M17, AK-47 (as the bridge), interceptor drone.

### 3.4 Where the visuals legally come from
| Source | License | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| **US patents** | US patent drawings are generally public domain 🟡 | Authentic historical drawings: Colt (1836), Gatling (1862), Maxim (1884), Browning, Stoner |
| **DVIDS** (US military media) | Works of US federal employees are generally public domain 🟡 (check each item) | Modern photos & video of US systems |
| **Smithsonian Open Access** | CC0 for designated items ✅ | Photos, some 3D scans |
| **Wikimedia Commons, Sketchfab** | Mixed CC licenses: check each 🟡 | Museum scans, historical photos |
| **Conflict Armament Research** | Reports with teardown photos, usage terms ⚠️ | Reference for Shahed/missile internals (ask permission) |
| **Our own work** | Ours | Blender models, commissioned illustrations, our own photos & museum photogrammetry (with permission) |
| **Company sites (Anduril, Beretta, SIG…)** | ❌ copyrighted | **Reference only**, never reused |

## 4. Tone rules (from all of the above)
1. Restraint (Helsing): no explosions, camo or "tactical" clichés.
2. Precision (newsrooms): every number sourced and dated.
3. Plain words: "kill," "destroy," "civilian deaths" when true. No euphemisms.
4. Every glamour shot is paired with a cost: "Who paid," right next to the specs.
5. Company claims are labeled as company claims.
